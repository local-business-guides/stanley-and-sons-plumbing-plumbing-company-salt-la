What to ask about the job

Plumbing work begins with understanding the concern and the area involved. The service needed depends on what is found, so don't assume a particular repair is included until you've discussed it with the company. Ask whether the proposed work addresses the cause or only a visible symptom, and whether any additional tasks may be needed.

Key facts for your call:
- Coverage: Ask which inspection, repair, or other tasks are part of the proposed service. Confirm what is outside that scope.
- Timing: The schedule and length of a job depend on the issue and the work required. Ask when an assessment may happen and what could affect completion.
- Cost factors: The type of problem, access, parts, and amount of labor can affect the total. Ask how added work or changed findings will be explained.
- Comparing providers: Get each provider to describe the same job and clarify what their stated cost includes. A useful comparison looks at scope and communication as well as the amount.

A few terms may come up. Assessment is checking the situation to understand the problem. Scope of work is the set of tasks included in the plan. Parts are the materials or components used for the job. If a provider uses a term you don't recognize, ask for a plain explanation.
